R1 Nature of the Tribunal's decision — rule 35
Four separate blocks on the page, plus one heading. SI 2026/115, in force 2 March 2026.
Do this. The page sets rule 35 out across four quoted blocks under their own headings. Three of them need amending, one heading needs renaming, and three new sub-points follow. The 28-day period for requesting full findings and reasons is now 14 days, and it is the gateway to permission to appeal.
Current text — to be deleted

Under the opening quotation of r.35(2)

"(2) The Tribunal must provide to each party within 28 days after making a decision (other than a decision under Part 4) which finally disposes of all issues in proceedings or of a preliminary issue dealt with following a direction under rule 5(3)(e), or as soon as practicable thereafter, a decision notice which-- (a) states the Tribunal's decision; and (b) notifies the party of any right of appeal against the decision and the time within which, and the manner in which, the right of appeal may be exercised." (FTT Rules, r.35(2))

Under "- Full reasons (necessary to appeal)"

"(3) Unless each party agrees that it is unnecessary, the decision notice must-- (a) include a summary of the findings of fact and reasons for the decision; or (b) be accompanied by full written findings of fact and reasons for the decision." (FTT Rules, r.35(3))

- Must apply for full reasons within 28 days

"(5) An application under paragraph (4) must be made in writing and be sent or delivered to the Tribunal so that it is received within 28 days after the date that the Tribunal sent or otherwise provided the decision notice under paragraph (2) to the party making the application." (FTT Rules, r.35(5))

Replacement

Opening quotation of r.35(2)

"(2) The Tribunal must provide to each party as soon as reasonably practicable after making a decision (other than a decision under Part 4) which finally disposes of all issues in proceedings or of a preliminary issue dealt with following a direction under rule 5(3)(e), a decision notice which-- (a) states the Tribunal's decision; and (b) notifies the party of any right of appeal against the decision and the time within which, and the manner in which, the right of appeal may be exercised." (FTT Rules, r.35(2))

Under "- Full reasons (necessary to appeal)"

"(3) The decision notice must-- (a) include a summary of the findings of fact and reasons for the decision; or (b) be accompanied by full written findings of fact and reasons for the decision.

(3A) Paragraph (3) does not apply where the Tribunal has given reasons for the decision, whether summary or full, orally at a hearing." (FTT Rules, r.35(3) and (3A))

- Must apply for full reasons within 14 days

"(5) An application under paragraph (4) must be made in writing and be sent or delivered to the Tribunal so that it is received within 14 days after the date that the Tribunal sent or otherwise provided the decision notice under paragraph (2) to the party making the application." (FTT Rules, r.35(5))

Three new sub-points — nothing deleted

- 14 days to apply for full written findings and reasons

The period was reduced from 28 days to 14 days by SI 2026/115 with effect from 2 March 2026. Because an application under r.35(4) is in practice the precondition of an application for permission to appeal under r.39, the shortened period is the operative deadline in any case where the decision notice contains a summary of findings only.

- No written findings and reasons where reasons given orally

The former route by which the parties could agree that findings and reasons were unnecessary has gone. In its place, r.35(3A) disapplies the duty altogether where the Tribunal gave reasons orally at the hearing, whether in summary or in full. A party who wants a written statement in such a case must apply under r.35(4), within 14 days.

- Tribunal's own deadlines replaced by "as soon as reasonably practicable"

The fixed 28-day periods formerly imposed on the Tribunal by r.35(2) and r.35(6) were replaced with "as soon as reasonably practicable". There is accordingly no longer a date by which a party may expect the decision notice or the full statement, and no fixed period from which to measure delay.

See S9: Excessive delay to decision

Two things to leave alone. The quotation of r.35(4) needs no change. And the passage at [52]–[55] under "- FTT accepting late request for full reasons despite no good reasons for delay" repeatedly refers to the 28-day limit — but that is a judge speaking about the rule as it then stood, so it should stay exactly as it is. It would be worth adding a line beneath it noting that the period is now 14 days.

X4 Penalties for late return — late company tax return
"LATE COMPANY TAX RETURN" → "- Fixed penalty for initial default". FA 2026 s.265, filing dates on or after 1 April 2026.
Do this. Four figures change inside the quotation. Sub-paragraphs (5) to (7) are new and are added at the end of it. Then two new sub-points follow. Nothing else in the chapter is affected — the tax-geared penalty in para 18 is unchanged.
Current text — to be deleted

"(1) A company which is required to deliver a company tax return and fails to do so by the filing date is liable to a flat-rate penalty under this paragraph.

It may also be liable to a tax-related penalty under paragraph 18.

(2) The penalty is—

(a) £100, if the return is delivered within three months after the filing date, and

(b) £200, in any other case.

(3) The amounts are increased to £500 and £1000 for a third successive failure, that is, where—

(a) the company is within the charge to corporation tax for three consecutive accounting periods …

(d) the company is again liable to a penalty under this paragraph in respect of the third period." (FA 1998, Sch 18, para 17)

Replacement

"(1) A company which is required to deliver a company tax return and fails to do so by the filing date is liable to a flat-rate penalty under this paragraph.

It may also be liable to a tax-related penalty under paragraph 18.

(2) The penalty is—

(a) £200, if the return is delivered within three months after the filing date, and

(b) £400, in any other case.

(3) The amounts are increased to £1000 and £2000 for a third successive failure, that is, where—

(a) the company is within the charge to corporation tax for three consecutive accounting periods … [unchanged]

(d) the company is again liable to a penalty under this paragraph in respect of the third period.

(5) The Commissioners for His Majesty's Revenue and Customs may by regulations amend sub-paragraph (2) or (3) so as to increase or decrease the amount of a penalty for the time being specified in those sub-paragraphs.

(6) Regulations under sub-paragraph (5) may include transitional and saving provision.

(7) A statutory instrument containing regulations under sub-paragraph (5) which increase the amount of a penalty by more than is necessary to reflect changes in the value of money may not be made unless a draft of the instrument has been laid before and approved by a resolution of the House of Commons." (FA 1998, Sch 18, para 17)

Two new sub-points — nothing deleted

- Amounts doubled for filing dates on or after 1 April 2026

"(2) The amendments made by subsection (1)(a) and (b) have effect in relation to a failure to deliver a company tax return for which the filing date is on or after 1 April 2026." (FA 2026, s.265(2))

For a filing date before 1 April 2026 the penalties remain £100 and £200, increased to £500 and £1,000 for a third successive failure. The trigger is the filing date, not the date of the failure or of the penalty determination, so the two sets of figures run alongside one another for as long as late returns for earlier periods are being dealt with.

- Power to vary the amounts by regulations

Sub-paragraphs (5) to (7) were inserted by FA 2026, s.265(1)(c). The affirmative procedure bites only on a real-terms increase; an inflation-only uprating may be made by negative instrument.

Confidence. The strongest item in the set — para 17 as amended returned identically from two independent routes and matches, figure for figure, what s.265 says it substitutes.

MAXIMUM SENTENCES

Maxima doubled for offences committed on or after 22 February 2024

FA 2024, s.32 increased the maximum term of imprisonment on conviction on indictment from 7 years to 14 years across more than twenty enactments, including TMA 1970, CEMA 1979, VATA 1994 and FA 2003. The increase applies by reference to the date of the offence, not the date of charge, conviction or sentence.

V14 Construction industry scheme — cancellation of gross payment status
"- No re-registering for 1 year after cancellation". FA 2026 s.220, from 6 April 2026.
Do this. More than a figure change. The page quotes s.66(8), and that subsection no longer exists — FA 2026 renumbered the bar into s.66(6)(b) for ordinary compliance cancellations and s.66(7)(b) for fraud and knowledge cancellations, and it is five years in the second case. So one sub-point becomes two, and the quoted subsection number changes.
Current text — to be deleted

- No re-registering for 1 year after cancellation

"(8) A person whose registration for gross payment is cancelled under this section may not, within the period of one year after the cancellation takes effect (see subsections (2) and (4) and section 67(5)), apply for registration for gross payment." (FA 2004, s.66(8))

The subsection quoted has been omitted, so both the number and the substance are now wrong for cancellations on the fraud and knowledge grounds.

Replacement — one sub-point becomes three

- Cancellation where s.62A or s.62B applies

"(3A) The Commissioners may at any time make a determination cancelling a person's registration for gross payment if— (a) section 62A (payments made in the knowledge of deliberate failures to comply), or (b) section 62B (returns made in the knowledge of deliberate failures to comply), applies to the person.

(4) Where the Commissioners make a determination under subsection (3) or subsection (3A), the person's registration for gross payment is cancelled with immediate effect." (FA 2004, s.66)

- No re-registering for 1 year following cancellation under s.66(1)

"(6) Where a person's registration for gross payment is cancelled by virtue of a determination under subsection (1), (a) the person must be registered for payment under deduction, and (b) the person may not, within the period of one year beginning with the day on which the cancellation takes effect (see subsection (2) and section 67(5)), apply for registration for gross payment." (FA 2004, s.66(6))

- No re-registering for 5 years following cancellation under s.66(3) or (3A)

"(7) Where a person's registration for gross payment is cancelled by virtue of a determination under subsection (3) or subsection (3A), (a) the person may, if the Commissioners think fit, be registered for payment under deduction, and (b) the person may not, within the period of five years beginning with the day on which the cancellation takes effect (see subsection (4)) apply for registration for gross payment." (FA 2004, s.66(7))

The five-year period replaced one year with effect from 6 April 2026. Cancellation on the ordinary compliance ground under s.66(1) carries a one-year bar; cancellation on the fraud or knowledge grounds under s.66(3) or (3A) carries five.

New category section — nothing deleted

LIABILITY FOR DELIBERATE FAILURES BY A CONNECTED PARTY

Payments made in the knowledge of deliberate failures to comply

"(1) This section applies to a person who— (a) has made a payment under a construction contract, and (b) before making a payment, knew or should have known that a connected party had deliberately failed, or would deliberately fail, to comply with a requirement to— (i) deduct a sum under section 61, (ii) pay a sum to the Commissioners under section 62, or (iii) deduct or pay an amount to His Majesty's Revenue and Customs under PAYE regulations.

(2) If this section applies, an officer of Revenue and Customs may determine that the person is liable to pay to the Commissioners an amount equal to 20% of the payment referred to in subsection (1).

(3) In this section, a "connected party" is— (a) another party to the construction contract referred to in subsection (1)(a), or (b) a party to another construction contract relating to the same construction operations as the construction contract referred to in subsection (1)(a)." (FA 2004, s.62A)

- "Knew or should have known"

The formula is that of the Kittel line in VAT, and the same evidential territory should be expected: what the payer actually knew, what enquiries a reasonable payer in its position would have made, and whether the only reasonable explanation for the arrangement was that the connected party would default. The reach of "connected party" is wide — not confined to the payer's own counterparty, but extending to any party to any construction contract relating to the same construction operations.

Returns made in the knowledge of deliberate failures to comply

"(1) This section applies to a person who— (a) makes a return which treats a sum as deducted and paid on account of the person's liabilities under section 62(2) or (3), and (b) before doing so, knew or should have known that the sum— (i) had not been deducted, or (ii) had deliberately not been, or would deliberately not be, paid on account of the person's liabilities.

(2) If this section applies, an officer of Revenue and Customs may determine that the person is liable to pay to the Commissioners an amount equal to the sum which the return treats as paid on account of the person's liabilities." (FA 2004, s.62B)

Penalty

"(1) A person is liable to a penalty not exceeding 30% of any amount that they are determined to be liable to pay under section 62A (payments made in the knowledge of deliberate failures to comply) or 62B (returns made in the knowledge of deliberate failures to comply).

(2) A penalty under this section may not be determined more than three years after the date on which the determination under section 62A or 62B becomes final.

(4) Section 103(4) TMA 1970 (time limits) does not apply to a penalty under this section." (FA 2004, s.72A)

- Commencement

ss.62A to 62C and ss.72A to 72C were inserted by FA 2026, s.220 with effect from 6 April 2026. Officer liability is dealt with by s.72B and appeals by s.72C.

Leave the case quotation alone. Under "Purpose is deterrence" the page quotes a judgment at [34] referring to "s 66(8) FA 2004" and the one-year period. That is the judge's language and should stay as it is, with a note beneath recording the renumbering and the five-year period.

Two drafting checks. s.72A(3) is omitted above because my copy reads "any appeal or final appeal" where "further appeal" is expected — a likely conversion slip. And there is no subsection (8) in s.66 as I have it; s.220 appears to have omitted it, which is consistent with the renumbering, but worth confirming.

X9 Special circumstances and suspension — Cox
Three citations of "Cox v. HMRC [2024] UKFTT 510 (TC), Judge Gemmell".
Do this. This is the one place where I would not delete the existing quotation. The FTT passage is a fair statement of the position as it then stood and reads naturally against Testa, Fane and Webb. What is needed is the Upper Tribunal's answer set beside it — because the UT rejected exactly the link the FTT drew.
Current text — retain, but no longer as the last word

"[290] The tribunal agrees with the Testa Tribunal at [32] that the use of the word 'further' in paragraph 14(3) implies a link between the type of inaccuracy for which the original penalty has been levied and the type of inaccuracy which might give rise to further penalties and with the Fane Tribunal and the Webb Tribunal decisions that a 'one off' error would not normally be suitable for a suspended penalty." (Cox v. HMRC [2024] UKFTT 510 (TC), Judge Gemmell)

That decision was appealed. The Upper Tribunal disagreed on both limbs.

Add beneath it

- Overturned on appeal

On appeal the Upper Tribunal held that "further" in para 14(3) does no more than describe another penalty for careless inaccuracy that might arise in the future, and imports no requirement that the future inaccuracy be similar to the original one; and that a one-off error is not automatically unsuitable for suspension, although its nature remains relevant to the exercise of the discretion.

(Philip and Debra Cox v. HMRC [2026] UKUT 7 (TCC))

- Conditions must do more than require accurate returns

A condition amounting to no more than an obligation to file accurate returns would render para 14(6) redundant. Conditions must address the underlying behaviour, represent an improvement on previous practice and meet the SMART criteria. The tribunal's jurisdiction on a refusal to suspend remains the "flawed" test in the public law sense.

- Reform in prospect

HMRC's summary of responses on behavioural penalties reform, published 26 November 2025, records an intention to replace suspension with a warning for a first careless inaccuracy. No draft legislation has been published and no implementation date given, but if enacted this chapter will need rewriting and Cox will become a transitional authority.

Headings to re-cast. "- Link to avoiding similar inaccuracies just a factor" and "- Not likely appropriate for one off errors/transactions" should both be read subject to the Upper Tribunal, and the three propositions currently sourced to the FTT decision re-sourced to it. A quotation from the UT judgment would be better than my summary above — it is on the list of judgments I still need.
