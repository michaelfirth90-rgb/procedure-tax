i16: Anti-avoidance information notices

THE NOTICES

Power to obtain information from a connected person

  • Notice requiring information reasonably required to monitor the connected person’s compliance, or for HMRC to consider action
  • The officer may seek tribunal approval before issuing the notice

Meaning of connected person

  • A person reasonably suspected of contravening an anti-avoidance enactment, or connected to such a person or to the arrangements
  • When two persons are connected
  • When a person is connected to arrangements

Meaning of anti-avoidance enactment

  • DOTAS, POTAS, the enablers penalty, indirect tax DOTAS and the new prohibition on promotion
  • Taking action under an anti-avoidance enactment includes director disqualification, joint and several liability, winding up, publication, freezing orders and promoter action notices

Power to obtain information from a third party

  • Notice to a third party for information about a connected person
  • The notice must identify the connected person
  • Advance notification of the intention to issue and of the information required, and a reasonable opportunity to make representations
  • No notice without the connected person’s agreement or the approval of the tribunal
  • The tribunal must be given a summary of any representations before approving
  • The connected person must be given a copy of the notice and a summary of the reasons
  • Those safeguards fall away so far as the tribunal is satisfied they might prejudice the investigation

Power to obtain information about an unidentified connected person

  • Notice about a person whose identity is not known
  • Meaning of unidentified connected person, including a class of persons
  • Only information not readily available from another source
  • Tribunal approval is always required
  • Action against a class includes action against one or more of its members

Power to obtain information to identify a connected person

  • Notice requiring identifying information to identify an unidentified connected person
  • The officer must have reason to believe the recipient could identify the person and obtained the information in the course of a business
  • Only information not readily available from another source
  • Identifying information means name, last known address and, for an individual, date of birth
  • An officer who is not an authorised officer needs the approval of the tribunal or of an authorised officer
  • An authorised officer may seek tribunal approval

Power to obtain information from a financial institution

  • Notice to a financial institution for information about a connected person
  • The notice must identify the connected person
  • Tribunal approval is always required
  • The connected person must be given a copy of the notice and a summary of the reasons
  • That safeguard falls away so far as the tribunal is satisfied it might prejudice the investigation
  • Meaning of financial institution

CONTENT, RESTRICTIONS AND WITHDRAWAL

What the notice must specify, and what it may require

  • What the notice must specify or describe, including whether it was approved by the tribunal
  • Only information that would not, in the reasonable opinion of an officer, be unduly onerous to provide
  • No document originating wholly more than 6 years before the notice without an authorised officer’s agreement
  • A notice may be issued to a person outside the United Kingdom

Notice may prohibit disclosure of its existence or contents

  • The notice may prohibit disclosure of its existence or contents to the connected person, the class, anyone likely to tell them, or any other person
  • A prohibition on disclosure to any other person cannot prevent complying with the notice or seeking legal advice
  • The restriction lasts 12 months unless withdrawn or extended
  • Withdrawal by notifying the recipient in writing
  • Extension by further periods of 12 months, on one or more occasions
  • Extension only where failure to extend might prejudice the investigation, and with an authorised officer’s agreement

Information a notice cannot require

  • Nothing outside the person’s possession or power
  • Pending appeals, personal records, journalistic material and privileged communications
  • Auditors: information held or created in performing the audit function
  • The auditor exception does not apply to an identification notice or to identifying information
  • Regulations for the tribunal to resolve disputes about privilege

Tribunal approval: procedure, and the decision is final

  • The application may be made without notice
  • An officer who is not an authorised officer needs the agreement of one before applying
  • The tribunal must be satisfied that the requirements of the relevant section are met
  • The tribunal’s decision is final, despite sections 11 and 13 of TCEA 2007

Withdrawal of a notice

  • An officer may withdraw a notice by notifying the recipient in writing

CRIMINAL SANCTIONS

Offence of failing to comply, or providing inaccurate information

  • Offence of failing to comply, or of carelessly or deliberately providing inaccurate information
  • Reasonable excuse is a defence to failing to comply, but not to providing inaccurate information
  • Carelessness means a failure to take reasonable care
  • The offence does not apply to a financial institution notice

Offence of concealing information

  • Offence of concealing, destroying or disposing of information required by the notice
  • Defence that it was done only after the information had been provided
  • That defence is lost where HMRC had notified that the information must remain available
  • Offence of destroying information after notification of an intended third party notice
  • Defences: six months after the last notification, or after the notice has been issued
  • Includes arranging for the concealment, destruction or disposal
  • The offence does not apply to a financial institution notice

Directors, members and partners are liable for the body’s offence

  • An offence committed with the consent or connivance of, or attributable to the neglect of, a responsible person is committed by that person too
  • Meaning of responsible person: directors, shadow directors, LLP members and partners
  • Meaning of shadow member
  • Where the recipient is a body corporate or partnership, the responsible person commits an offence even though the recipient is not prosecuted
  • Reasonable excuse of the responsible person or of the recipient is a defence
  • Does not apply to a financial institution notice

Punishment on conviction

  • A fine on summary conviction, or up to two years’ imprisonment or a fine on indictment

CIVIL SANCTIONS

Penalty for failing to comply with a notice

  • Penalty for failing to comply without reasonable excuse
  • £5,000, or £300 for a financial institution notice
  • Daily penalty for each day the failure continues after a penalty is imposed
  • Up to £1,000 a day, or £60 for a financial institution notice
  • Notification and 30 days to make representations before a penalty is imposed
  • Does not apply to breach of a disclosure restriction

Penalty for concealing information

  • Penalty for concealing, destroying or disposing of the information before it is provided, or after being told it must remain available
  • Penalty where information is destroyed after notification of an intended third party notice
  • Not liable where it was done only after six months, or after the notice was issued
  • £20,000, or £300 for a financial institution notice
  • Includes arranging for the concealment, destruction or disposal

Penalty for inaccurate information

  • Penalty for careless or deliberate inaccuracy, or for failing to take reasonable steps to notify a discovered inaccuracy
  • £20,000 for each inaccuracy, or £3,000 for a financial institution notice
  • Notification and 30 days to make representations
  • Carelessness means a failure to take reasonable care

Penalty for disclosing a notice

  • Penalty for breaching a disclosure restriction without reasonable excuse
  • £10,000, or £1,000 for a financial institution notice

Penalty based on monies received

  • Available only on a continuing failure, where money was received in connection with the arrangements and the Upper Tribunal decides it is appropriate
  • When a continuing failure is significant: six months, or significantly greater difficulty for HMRC
  • The penalty equals the money or money’s worth received in connection with the arrangements
  • The amount is determined by the Upper Tribunal, and that determination is treated as an assessment
  • Application within 12 months, and the person must be notified
  • Meaning of contravening arrangements

Increased daily default penalty

  • Application to the tribunal where the failure continues 30 days after the daily penalty was notified
  • The tribunal determines the amount and the day from which it applies
  • Maximum £5,000 a day, or £1,000 for a financial institution notice
  • The tribunal must have regard to the cost of compliance and the benefits of non-compliance to the person and to anyone else
  • The person must be notified of the amount and the day

SANCTIONS: GENERAL

Time allowed by HMRC or the tribunal is disregarded

  • A failure is disregarded if the thing is done within such further time as HMRC or the tribunal allowed

Reasonable excuse

  • Insufficiency of funds, reliance on another person, an excuse that has ceased, and reliance on legal advice

Double jeopardy

  • No penalty where the person has been convicted of an offence in respect of the same thing

Schedule 36 to FA 2008 applied to assessment and enforcement

  • Schedule 36 paragraph 46 applied to the penalties for failing to comply, concealing information and inaccurate information
  • Paragraph 51B(2) and (3) applied to the penalty for disclosing a notice
  • Paragraph 49 applied to enforcement of the penalties
  • Paragraph 51 applied to the penalty based on monies received

APPEALS

Appealing a notice

  • Appeal against a connected person notice, or any requirement in it, on any ground
  • Appeal against a third party notice only on the ground that compliance would be unduly onerous
  • Appeal against an identification notice only on the ground that compliance would be unduly onerous
  • No appeal at all where the notice was issued with tribunal approval
  • Schedule 36 paragraph 32 applied to appeals against notices

Appealing a penalty

  • Appeal against liability and against amount
  • Schedule 36 paragraph 48 applied to the procedure on appeal

INTERPRETATION AND REPEALS

Interpretation

  • Definitions applying throughout the Chapter
  • Providing information includes producing documents, and the Schedule 36 provisions about documents are applied

Application of provisions of TMA 1970

  • Responsibility of officers, want of form, and delivery and service of documents

Repeal of section 272A of FA 2014

  • Section 272A of FA 2014 repealed, on a day to be appointed by regulations (not in force at 18 March 2026)

Power to obtain information from a connected person

​

- Notice requiring information reasonably required to monitor the connected person’s compliance, or for HMRC to consider action

​

"(1) An officer of Revenue and Customs may by notice require a connected person to provide information that is, in the opinion of the officer, reasonably required for the purposes of—

(a) monitoring the compliance of the connected person with an anti-avoidance enactment, or

(b) HMRC taking, or considering whether HMRC could take, action against the connected person under an anti-avoidance enactment." (FA 2026, s.179(1))

​

- The officer may seek tribunal approval before issuing the notice

​

"(2) An officer of Revenue and Customs may seek the approval of the tribunal before issuing a notice under this section." (FA 2026, s.179(2))

​

Meaning of connected person

​

- A person reasonably suspected of contravening an anti-avoidance enactment, or connected to such a person or to the arrangements

​

"(1) In this Chapter, a “ connected person ” means a person who an officer of Revenue and Customs reasonably suspects is or has been—

(a) contravening an anti-avoidance enactment,

(b) connected to a person who is or has been contravening an anti-avoidance enactment, or

(c) connected to arrangements by reference to which a person is or has been contravening an anti-avoidance enactment." (FA 2026, s.177(1))

​

- When two persons are connected

​

"(2) For the purposes of subsection (1) (b), two persons (“ A ” and “ B ”) are connected if—

(a) A is a director, manager, secretary or other officer or employee of B,

(b) A is a member of, or partner in, B,

(c) A is a trustee, settlor, beneficiary or administrator of a trust in respect of which B is a trustee, settlor, beneficiary or administrator, or

(d) A is accustomed to acting in accordance with B’s directions or instructions." (FA 2026, s.177(2))

​

- When a person is connected to arrangements

​

"(3) For the purposes of subsection (1) (c), a person is connected to arrangements if the person is—

(a) to any extent involved in making the arrangements available for implementation by another person,

(b) to any extent involved in the organisation or management of the arrangements, or

(c) directly or indirectly benefiting from the arrangements." (FA 2026, s.177(3))

​

Meaning of anti-avoidance enactment

​

- DOTAS, POTAS, the enablers penalty, indirect tax DOTAS and the new prohibition on promotion

​

"(1) In this Chapter, an “ anti-avoidance enactment ” means—

(a) Part 7 of FA 2004 (disclosure of tax avoidance schemes);

(b) Part 5 of FA 2014 (promoters of tax avoidance schemes);

(c) Schedule 16 to F(No.2)A 2017 (penalties for enablers of defeated tax avoidance);

(d) Schedule 17 to F(No.2)A 2017 (disclosure of tax avoidance schemes: VAT and other indirect taxes);

(e) Chapter 1 of this Part (prohibition on promotion of certain tax avoidance arrangements)." (FA 2026, s.178(1))

​

- Taking action under an anti-avoidance enactment includes director disqualification, joint and several liability, winding up, publication, freezing orders and promoter action notices

​

"(2) And a reference to taking action under an anti-avoidance enactment includes a reference to taking action under—

(a) sections 8ZF and 8ZG of the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 (disqualification for promoting tax avoidance);

(b) Schedule 13 to FA 2020 (joint and several liability);

(c) section 85 of FA 2022 (winding up petitions by an officer of HMRC);

(d) section 86 of FA 2022 (publication by HMRC of information about tax avoidance schemes);

(e) sections 87, 88 and 89 of FA 2022 (freezing orders etc);

(f) Schedule 13 to FA 2022 (penalties for facilitating avoidance schemes involving non-resident promoters);

(g) Chapter 2 of this Part (promoter action notices). Notices by type" (FA 2026, s.178(2))

​

Power to obtain information from a third party

​

- Notice to a third party for information about a connected person

​

"(1) An officer of Revenue and Customs may by notice require a person to provide information that is, in the opinion of the officer, reasonably required for the purposes of—

(a) monitoring the compliance of a connected person with an anti-avoidance enactment, or

(b) HMRC taking, or considering whether HMRC could take, action against a connected person under an anti-avoidance enactment." (FA 2026, s.180(1))

​

- The notice must identify the connected person

​

"(2) A notice under this section must identify the connected person to whom it relates." (FA 2026, s.180(2))

​

- Advance notification of the intention to issue and of the information required, and a reasonable opportunity to make representations

​

"(3) Before issuing a notice under this section, an officer of Revenue and Customs must—

(a) notify the intended recipient of—

(i) the officer’s intention to issue the notice, and

(ii) the information that would be required under the notice, and

(b) allow the intended recipient reasonable opportunity to make representations to HMRC." (FA 2026, s.180(3))

​

- No notice without the connected person’s agreement or the approval of the tribunal

​

"(4) An officer of Revenue and Customs may not issue a notice under this section without either—

(a) the agreement of the connected person identified in the notice, or

(b) the approval of the tribunal." (FA 2026, s.180(4))

​

- The tribunal must be given a summary of any representations before approving

​

"(5) The tribunal may not approve a notice unless it has been given a summary of any representations made under subsection (3) (b)." (FA 2026, s.180(5))

​

- The connected person must be given a copy of the notice and a summary of the reasons

​

"(6) After issuing a notice under this section, an officer of Revenue and Customs must provide to the connected person identified in the notice—

(a) a copy of the notice, and

(b) a summary of the officer’s reasons for requiring the information." (FA 2026, s.180(6))

​

- Those safeguards fall away so far as the tribunal is satisfied they might prejudice the investigation

​

"(7) Subsections (2), (3) and (6) do not apply to the extent the tribunal is satisfied that taking the steps in those subsections might prejudice the investigation of tax avoidance." (FA 2026, s.180(7))

- Only information not readily available from another source

- Action against a class includes action against one or more of its members

"(5) In subsection (1) (b), the reference to taking action against an unidentified connected person includes a reference to taking action against one or more members of a class referred to in subsection (2) (b)." (FA 2026, s.181(5))

"(2) An officer of Revenue and Customs may not issue a notice under subsection (1) unless the officer has reason to believe that—

(a) the intended recipient of the notice could identify the unidentified connected person by reference to information provided by the officer, and

"(4) In this section — “ identifying information ” means one or more of an unidentified connected person’s— (a) name; (b) last known address; (c) in the case of an individual, date of birth; “ unidentified connected person ” has the meaning given in section 181." (FA 2026, s.182(4))

- An officer who is not an authorised officer needs the approval of the tribunal or of an authorised officer

"(5) An officer of Revenue and Customs who is not an authorised officer may not issue a notice under this section without the approval of either—

(a) the tribunal, or

(b) an authorised officer of Revenue and Customs." (FA 2026, s.182(5))

"(6) An authorised officer of Revenue and Customs may seek the approval of the tribunal before issuing a notice under this section." (FA 2026, s.182(6))

CONTENT, RESTRICTIONS AND WITHDRAWAL

What the notice must specify, and what it may require

​

- What the notice must specify or describe, including whether it was approved by the tribunal

​

"(1) An information notice must specify (or, in the case of paragraphs (a) and (b), describe)—

(a) the information that the recipient is required to provide;

(b) the form in which, and the means by which, the information is to be provided;

(c) a reasonable period within which the information is to be provided;

(d) the provision under which the notice is issued;

(e) whether the notice is issued with the approval of the tribunal." (FA 2026, s.184(1))

​

- Only information that would not, in the reasonable opinion of an officer, be unduly onerous to provide

​

"(2) An information notice issued under sections 180 to 183 may only require information that would not, in the reasonable opinion of an officer of Revenue and Customs, be unduly onerous for the recipient to provide." (FA 2026, s.184(2))

​

- No document originating wholly more than 6 years before the notice without an authorised officer’s agreement

​

"(3) An information notice may not require a person to produce a document if the whole of the document originates more than 6 years before the date of the notice without the agreement of an authorised officer of Revenue and Customs." (FA 2026, s.184(3))

​

- A notice may be issued to a person outside the United Kingdom

​

"(4) An information notice may be issued to a person outside the United Kingdom." (FA 2026, s.184(4))

​

Notice may prohibit disclosure of its existence or contents

​

- The notice may prohibit disclosure of its existence or contents to the connected person, the class, anyone likely to tell them, or any other person

​

"(1) An information notice may require the recipient not to disclose the existence or contents of the notice to—

(a) the connected person to whom the notice relates,

(b) in the case of a notice under section 181, members of the class of persons to which the notice relates,

(c) any person who might reasonably be expected to disclose the existence or contents of the notice to the connected person or members of the class, or

(d) any other person." (FA 2026, s.185(1))

​

- A prohibition on disclosure to any other person cannot prevent complying with the notice or seeking legal advice

​

"(2) A requirement under subsection (1) (d) may not prohibit disclosure for, or in connection with, the purpose of—

(a) complying with the notice, or

(b) seeking legal advice." (FA 2026, s.185(2))

​

- The restriction lasts 12 months unless withdrawn or extended

​

"(3) A requirement imposed under subsection (1) has effect until the end of the period of 12 months beginning with the day on which the notice is issued, unless before the end of that period—

(a) the requirement is withdrawn in accordance with subsection (4), or

(b) the period is extended in accordance with subsection (5)." (FA 2026, s.185(3))

​

- Withdrawal by notifying the recipient in writing

​

"(4) An officer of Revenue and Customs may withdraw the requirement by notifying the recipient in writing." (FA 2026, s.185(4))

​

- Extension by further periods of 12 months, on one or more occasions

​

"(5) An officer of Revenue and Customs may by notice to the recipient—

(a) extend the period during which a requirement imposed under subsection (1) has effect by a period of 12 months beginning with the day after the last day of the previous period of 12 months, and

(b) do so on one or more occasions." (FA 2026, s.185(5))

​

- Extension only where failure to extend might prejudice the investigation, and with an authorised officer’s agreement

​

"(6) An officer of Revenue and Customs may not issue a notice under subsection (5) unless—

(a) the officer considers that there are reasonable grounds for believing that failure to extend the period might prejudice the investigation of tax avoidance, and

(b) where the officer is not an authorised officer of Revenue and Customs, an authorised officer agrees with the officer’s—

(i) decision to extend the period, and

(ii) assessment under paragraph (a)." (FA 2026, s.185(6))

Information a notice cannot require

​

- Nothing outside the person’s possession or power

​

"(1) An information notice does not require a person to provide information that is not in the possession or power of that person." (FA 2026, s.186(1))

​

- Pending appeals, personal records, journalistic material and privileged communications

​

"(2) An information notice does not require a person to provide—

(a) information that relates to the conduct of a pending tax appeal or appeal against a decision under an anti-avoidance enactment;

(b) personal records (as defined in section 12 of the Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984) or information contained in such records, except that a notice may require a person to produce a redacted version of a document omitting any information that would otherwise make it personal records;

(c) journalistic material (as defined in section 13 of that Act) or information contained in such material;

(d) information in respect of a which a claim to legal professional privilege or, (in Scotland) to confidentiality of communications as between a client and professional legal advisor, could be maintained by the person in legal proceedings." (FA 2026, s.186(2))

- Auditors: information held or created in performing the audit function

​

"(3) An information notice does not require a person who has been appointed auditor for the purposes of an enactment to provide information held or created in connection with the performance of the person’s functions under the enactment, other than information that the recipient of the notice has assisted any client in preparing for, or delivering to, HMRC." (FA 2026, s.186(3))

​

- The auditor exception does not apply to an identification notice or to identifying information

​

"(4) Subsection (3) does not apply in relation to—

(a) a notice under section 182 (identification), or

(b) identifying information (within the meaning given in section 182) required by a notice under section 181 in respect of—

(i) the connected person to whom the notice relates, or

(ii) a person who has acted on behalf of the connected person." (FA 2026, s.186(4))

​

- Regulations for the tribunal to resolve disputes about privilege

​

"(5) The Commissioners may by regulations make provision for the resolution by the tribunal of disputes as to whether any information falls within subsection (2) (d) (privilege).

(6) Regulations under subsection (5) are to be made by statutory instrument and a statutory instrument containing regulations under subsection (5) is subject to annulment in pursuance of a resolution of the House of Commons." (FA 2026, s.186(5), (6))

Tribunal approval: procedure, and the decision is final

​

- The application may be made without notice

​

"(1) An application to the tribunal for approval of a notice, or disapplication of requirements, under this Chapter may be made without notice." (FA 2026, s.187(1))

​

- An officer who is not an authorised officer needs the agreement of one before applying

​

"(2) An officer of Revenue and Customs (other than an authorised officer) may not seek the approval of, or disapplication of requirements by, the tribunal without the agreement of an authorised officer of Revenue and Customs." (FA 2026, s.187(2))

​

- The tribunal must be satisfied that the requirements of the relevant section are met

​

"(3) The tribunal may not approve the issue of a notice under sections 179 to 183 unless it is satisfied that the requirements of the relevant section are met." (FA 2026, s.187(3))

​

- The tribunal’s decision is final, despite sections 11 and 13 of TCEA 2007

​

"(4) A decision of the tribunal is final (despite sections 11 and 13 of the Tribunals, Courts and Enforcement Act 2007)." (FA 2026, s.187(4))

Withdrawal of a notice

​

- An officer may withdraw a notice by notifying the recipient in writing

​

"An officer of Revenue and Customs may withdraw an information notice by notifying the recipient in writing." (FA 2026, s.188)

​

CRIMINAL SANCTIONS

Offence of failing to comply, or providing inaccurate information

​

- Offence of failing to comply, or of carelessly or deliberately providing inaccurate information

​

"(1) A recipient of an information notice commits an offence if the recipient—

(a) fails to comply with the notice, or

(b) in purporting to comply with the notice, carelessly or deliberately provides inaccurate information." (FA 2026, s.189(1))

​

- Reasonable excuse is a defence to failing to comply, but not to providing inaccurate information

​

"(2) It is a defence for a person charged with an offence under subsection (1) (a) to show that they had a reasonable excuse." (FA 2026, s.189(2))

​

- Carelessness means a failure to take reasonable care

​

"(3) In this section, a reference to carelessness is a reference to a failure to take reasonable care." (FA 2026, s.189(3))

​

- The offence does not apply to a financial institution notice

​

"(4) This section does not apply in respect of a notice under section 183 (financial institutions)." (FA 2026, s.189(4))

​

Offence of concealing information

​

- Offence of concealing, destroying or disposing of information required by the notice

​

"(1) A recipient of an information notice commits an offence if the recipient conceals, destroys or otherwise disposes of information that is required to be provided under the notice." (FA 2026, s.190(1))

​

- Defence that it was done only after the information had been provided

​

"(2) It is a defence for a person charged with an offence under subsection (1) to show that they concealed, destroyed or otherwise disposed of the information only after the information had been provided in accordance with the notice." (FA 2026, s.190(2))

​

- That defence is lost where HMRC had notified that the information must remain available

​

"(3) Subsection (2) does not apply where an officer of Revenue and Customs had notified the person in writing that the information must continue to be available (and had not withdrawn that notification)." (FA 2026, s.190(3))

​

- Offence of destroying information after notification of an intended third party notice

​

"(4) A person commits an offence if—

(a) an officer of Revenue and Customs has notified the person under section 180 (3) that—

(i) the officer intends to issue an information notice to the person, and

(ii) certain information would be required under the notice, and

(b) the person conceals, destroys or otherwise disposes of the information." (FA 2026, s.190(4))

​

- Defences: six months after the last notification, or after the notice has been issued

​

"(5) It is a defence for a person charged with an offence under subsection (4) to show that they concealed, destroyed or otherwise disposed of the information only—

(a) after the end of the period of six months beginning with the day on which they were last notified under section 180 (3) in respect of the information, or

(b) after an information notice has been issued to the person in respect of the information." (FA 2026, s.190(5))

​

- Includes arranging for the concealment, destruction or disposal

​

"(6) In this section, a reference to concealing, destroying or otherwise disposing of information includes a reference to arranging for the concealment, destruction or disposal of information." (FA 2026, s.190(6))

​

- The offence does not apply to a financial institution notice

​

"(7) This section does not apply in respect of a notice under section 183 (financial institutions)." (FA 2026, s.190(7))

​

Directors, members and partners are liable for the body’s offence

​

- An offence committed with the consent or connivance of, or attributable to the neglect of, a responsible person is committed by that person too

​

"(1) If an offence under section 189 or 190 is committed by a body corporate or a partnership and—

(a) is committed with the consent or connivance of a responsible person, or

(b) is attributable to the neglect of a responsible person, the responsible person commits the offence (as well as the body or partnership)." (FA 2026, s.191(1))

​

- Meaning of responsible person: directors, shadow directors, LLP members and partners

​

"(2) A “ responsible person ” means—

(a) in relation to a body corporate other than one whose affairs are managed by its members—

(i) a director, manager, secretary or other similar officer of the body, or a person purporting to act in such a capacity, or

(ii) a shadow director within the meaning given in section 251 of the Companies Act 2006;

(b) in relation to a limited liability partnership or other body corporate whose affairs are managed by its members—

(i) a member exercising management functions, or purporting to do so, or

(ii) in the case of a limited liability partnership, a shadow member;

(c) in relation to a partnership, a partner or a person purporting to act in that capacity." (FA 2026, s.191(2))

​

- Meaning of shadow member

​

"(3) In this section, a “ shadow member ” means a person in accordance with whose directions or instructions the members of the limited liability partnership are accustomed to act, save that a person is not a shadow member by reason only of the fact that the members act on advice given by that person in a professional capacity." (FA 2026, s.191(3))

​

- Where the recipient is a body corporate or partnership, the responsible person commits an offence even though the recipient is not prosecuted

​

"(1) Subsection (2) applies where a body corporate or partnership that is the recipient of an information notice—

(a) fails to comply with the notice, or

(b) in purporting to comply with the notice, carelessly or deliberately provides inaccurate information.

(2) If the recipient’s failure or careless or deliberate provision of inaccurate information—

(a) occurred with the consent or connivance of a responsible person, or

(b) is attributable to the neglect of a responsible person, the responsible person commits an offence." (FA 2026, s.192(1), (2))

​

- Reasonable excuse of the responsible person or of the recipient is a defence

​

"(3) It is a defence for a person charged with an offence under subsection (2) to show that they or the recipient had a reasonable excuse." (FA 2026, s.192(3))

​

- Does not apply to a financial institution notice

​

"(5) This section does not apply in respect of a notice under section 183 (financial institutions)." (FA 2026, s.192(5))

​

Punishment on conviction

​

- A fine on summary conviction, or up to two years’ imprisonment or a fine on indictment

​

"(a) on summary conviction, to—

(i) in England and Wales, a fine, or

(ii) in Scotland or Northern Ireland, a fine not exceeding the statutory maximum, or

(b) on conviction on indictment, to imprisonment for a term not exceeding two years or to a fine or both. Civil sanctions" (FA 2026, s.193)

​

CIVIL SANCTIONS

Penalty for failing to comply with a notice

​

- Penalty for failing to comply without reasonable excuse

​

"(1) A recipient of an information notice is liable to a penalty if the recipient—

(a) fails to comply with the notice, and

(b) does so without reasonable excuse." (FA 2026, s.194(1))

​

- £5,000, or £300 for a financial institution notice

​

"(2) The penalty under subsection (1) is—

(a) in relation to a notice under section 183 (financial institutions), £300;

(b) otherwise, £5,000." (FA 2026, s.194(2))

​

- Daily penalty for each day the failure continues after a penalty is imposed

​

"(3) If a failure referred to in subsection (1) continues after the day on which a penalty is imposed in respect of it, the recipient is liable to a further penalty for each day on which the failure continues." (FA 2026, s.194(3))

​

- Up to £1,000 a day, or £60 for a financial institution notice

​

"(4) The penalty under subsection (3) is—

(a) in relation to a notice under section 183 (financial institutions), an amount not exceeding £60;

(b) otherwise, an amount not exceeding £1,000." (FA 2026, s.194(4))

​

- Notification and 30 days to make representations before a penalty is imposed

​

"(5) Before imposing a penalty under this section, an officer of Revenue and Customs must—

(a) notify the recipient of the fact that the officer considers subsection (1) or (3) to apply, and

(b) allow the recipient a period of 30 days beginning with the date of notification to make representations to HMRC." (FA 2026, s.194(5))

​

- Does not apply to breach of a disclosure restriction

​

"(6) This section does not apply in relation to a failure to comply with a requirement imposed under section 185 (restriction on disclosure of notices)." (FA 2026, s.194(6))

​

Penalty for concealing information

​

- Penalty for concealing, destroying or disposing of the information before it is provided, or after being told it must remain available

​

"(1) A recipient of an information notice is liable to a penalty if the recipient—

(a) is required to provide information under the notice issued, and

(b) conceals, destroys or otherwise disposes of the information—

(i) before the information has been provided in accordance with the notice, or

(ii) in circumstances where an officer of Revenue and Customs has notified the person in writing that the information must continue to be available (and has not withdrawn that notification)." (FA 2026, s.195(1))

​

- Penalty where information is destroyed after notification of an intended third party notice

​

"(2) A person is liable to a penalty if—

(a) an officer of Revenue and Customs has notified the person under section 180 (3) that—

(i) the officer intends to issue an information notice to the person, and

(ii) certain information would be required under the notice, and

(b) the person conceals, destroys or otherwise disposes of the information." (FA 2026, s.195(2))

​

- Not liable where it was done only after six months, or after the notice was issued

​

"(3) Subsection (2) does not apply if the person concealed, destroyed or otherwise disposed of the information only—

(a) after the end of the period of 6 months beginning with the day on which they were last notified under section 180 (3) in respect of the information, or

(b) after an information notice has been issued to the person in respect of the information." (FA 2026, s.195(3))

​

- £20,000, or £300 for a financial institution notice

​

"(4) The penalty under subsection (1) or (2) is—

(a) in relation to a notice under section 183 (financial institutions), £300, or

(b) otherwise, £20,000." (FA 2026, s.195(4))

​

- Includes arranging for the concealment, destruction or disposal

​

"(5) In this section, a reference to concealing, destroying or otherwise disposing of information includes a reference to arranging for the concealment, destruction or disposal of information." (FA 2026, s.195(5))

​

Penalty for inaccurate information

​

- Penalty for careless or deliberate inaccuracy, or for failing to take reasonable steps to notify a discovered inaccuracy

​

"(1) A recipient of an information notice is liable to a penalty if the recipient—

(a) in purporting to comply with the notice, carelessly or deliberately provides inaccurate information, or

(b) after purporting to comply with the notice—

(i) discovers that, in doing so, they provided inaccurate information, and

(ii) does not take reasonable steps to notify HMRC of that fact." (FA 2026, s.196(1))

​

- £20,000 for each inaccuracy, or £3,000 for a financial institution notice

​

"(2) The maximum penalty under subsection (1) is—

(a) in relation to a notice under section 183 (financial institutions), £3,000 for each inaccuracy, or

(b) otherwise, £20,000 for each inaccuracy." (FA 2026, s.196(2))

​

- Notification and 30 days to make representations

​

"(3) Before imposing a penalty under this section, an officer of Revenue and Customs must—

(a) notify the recipient of the fact that the officer considers subsection (1) to apply, and

(b) allow the recipient a period of 30 days beginning with the date of notification to make representations to HMRC." (FA 2026, s.196(3))

​

- Carelessness means a failure to take reasonable care

​

"(4) In this section, a reference to carelessness is a reference to a failure to take reasonable care." (FA 2026, s.196(4))

​

Penalty for disclosing a notice

​

- Penalty for breaching a disclosure restriction without reasonable excuse

​

"(1) A recipient of an information notice is liable to a penalty if the recipient—

(a) fails to comply with a requirement imposed under section 185 (restriction on disclosure of notices), and

(b) does so without reasonable excuse." (FA 2026, s.197(1))

​

- £10,000, or £1,000 for a financial institution notice

​

"(2) The penalty under subsection (1) is—

(a) in relation to a notice under section 183 (financial institutions), £1,000, or

(b) otherwise, £10,000." (FA 2026, s.197(2))

​

Penalty based on monies received

​

- Available only on a continuing failure, where money was received in connection with the arrangements and the Upper Tribunal decides it is appropriate

​

"(1) Subsection (3) applies if—

(a) a penalty has been imposed on a person under section 194 (1) for failure to comply with a notice,

(b) the person, without reasonable excuse, continues to fail to comply with the notice,

(c) an officer of Revenue and Customs has reason to believe that—

(i) the person received money or money’s worth in connection with the contravening arrangements to which the notice relates, and

(ii) the continuing failure is significant, and

(d) the Upper Tribunal decides that it is appropriate for a penalty to be imposed under this section." (FA 2026, s.198(1))

​

- When a continuing failure is significant: six months, or significantly greater difficulty for HMRC

​

"(2) For the purposes of subsection (1) (c) (ii), a continuing failure is significant if—

(a) it continues beyond the end of the period of six months beginning with the day on which the penalty referred to in subsection (1) (a) was imposed, or

(b) as a result of the continuing failure, it is or is likely to be significantly more difficult for HMRC to—

(i) monitor the compliance of a connected person with an anti-avoidance enactment, or

(ii) take, or consider whether HMRC could take, action against a connected person under an anti-avoidance enactment." (FA 2026, s.198(2))

​

- The penalty equals the money or money’s worth received in connection with the arrangements

​

"(3) The person is liable to a penalty equal to the amount of money or money’s worth received, or likely to have been received, by the person in connection with the contravening arrangements to which the notice relates." (FA 2026, s.198(3))

​

- The amount is determined by the Upper Tribunal, and that determination is treated as an assessment

​

"(4) The amount of a penalty to which a person is liable under subsection (3) must be determined by the Upper Tribunal (with such determination being treated as assessment)." (FA 2026, s.198(4))

​

- Application within 12 months, and the person must be notified

​

"(5) An application to the Tribunal for the purposes of subsection (1) (d) or (4) may be made by an officer of Revenue and Customs and must be made before the end of the period of 12 months beginning with—

(a) in the case of a penalty relating to a notice against which the recipient may appeal under section 204, the latest of—

(i) the day on which the person became liable to a penalty under section 194 (1),

(ii) the last day of the period in which notice of appeal against the notice could have been given, and

(iii) if notice of such an appeal has been given, the day on which the appeal is determined or withdrawn, or

(b) in any other case, the day on which the person became liable to a penalty under section 194 (1).

(6) An officer of Revenue and Customs who makes an application for the purposes of subsection (1) (d) or (4) must notify the person concerned." (FA 2026, s.198(5), (6))

​

- Meaning of contravening arrangements

​

"(7) For the purposes of this section, a reference to contravening arrangements to which a notice relates is a reference to arrangements by reference to which the person to whom the notice relates is considered to be a connected person under section 177 (1) (including, where the person is a connected person under section 177 (1)(b), arrangements by reference to which the other person is considered to be a connected person)." (FA 2026, s.198(7))

​

Increased daily default penalty

​

- Application to the tribunal where the failure continues 30 days after the daily penalty was notified

​

"(1) An officer of Revenue and Customs may apply to the tribunal for a determination that an increased penalty should be available under section 194 (4) in respect of a person’s failure to comply with an information notice if—

(a) a penalty has been imposed under section 194 (3) (daily penalties for continuing failure) in respect of the failure,

(b) the failure continues after the end of the period of 30 days beginning with the day on which notification of the penalty under section 194 (3) was issued (see paragraph 46 to Schedule 36 to FA 2008), and

(c) the officer has notified the person of their intention to apply to the tribunal under this section." (FA 2026, s.199(1))

​

- The tribunal determines the amount and the day from which it applies

​

"(2) If the tribunal decides that an increased penalty should be available under section 194 (4) in respect of a person’s failure, the tribunal must determine—

(a) the amount of the increased penalty, and

(b) the day from which it is to be applicable.

(3) The increased penalty is available under section 194 (4) in respect of the failure—

(a) from the day determined by the tribunal, and

(b) as though the figure in that subsection were replaced with the amount of the increased penalty." (FA 2026, s.199(2), (3))

​

- Maximum £5,000 a day, or £1,000 for a financial institution notice

​

"(4) An increased penalty under this section may not exceed—

(a) in relation to a notice under section 183 (financial institutions), £1,000, or

(b) otherwise, £5,000." (FA 2026, s.199(4))

​

- The tribunal must have regard to the cost of compliance and the benefits of non-compliance to the person and to anyone else

​

"(5) In determining the amount of the increased penalty, the tribunal must have regard to—

(a) the likely cost to the person of complying with the notice,

(b) any benefits to the person of not complying with the notice, and

(c) any benefits to anyone else resulting from the person’s non-compliance." (FA 2026, s.199(5))

​

- The person must be notified of the amount and the day

​

"(6) If the tribunal makes a determination under subsection (2), an officer of Revenue and Customs must notify the person to whom it relates of—

(a) the amount of the increased penalty, and

(b) the day from which it is to be applicable. Sanctions: general" (FA 2026, s.199(6))

SANCTIONS: GENERAL

Time allowed by HMRC or the tribunal is disregarded

​

- A failure is disregarded if the thing is done within such further time as HMRC or the tribunal allowed

​

"For the purposes of sections 189 to 199 (sanctions), a failure of a person to do anything within a limited period of time is to be disregarded if the person did the thing within such further period of time, if any, as an officer of Revenue and Customs or the tribunal allowed." (FA 2026, s.200)

​

Reasonable excuse

​

- Insufficiency of funds, reliance on another person, an excuse that has ceased, and reliance on legal advice

​

"(a) an insufficiency of funds is not a reasonable excuse unless attributable to events outside the person’s control,

(b) if the person relies on any other person to do anything, that is not a reasonable excuse unless the first person took reasonable care to avoid the failure,

(c) if the person had a reasonable excuse for the failure but the excuse has ceased, the person is to be treated as having continued to have the excuse if the failure is remedied without unreasonable delay after the excuse ceased, and

(d) reliance on legal advice is to be taken automatically not to constitute a reasonable excuse if either—

(i) the advice was not based on a full and accurate description of the facts, or

(ii) the conclusions in the advice that the person relied upon were unreasonable." (FA 2026, s.201)

See A4: Reasonable excuse examples

Double jeopardy

​

- No penalty where the person has been convicted of an offence in respect of the same thing

​

"A person is not liable to a penalty under this Chapter in respect of anything in respect of which the person has been convicted of an offence." (FA 2026, s.202)

- Schedule 36 paragraph 46 applied to the penalties for failing to comply, concealing information and inaccurate information

"(2) Paragraph 46 (assessment of penalty) applies to—

(a) a penalty under sections 194 (failure to comply) and 195 (concealing information) as it applies to a penalty under paragraphs 39 and 40 of the Schedule;

(b) a penalty under section 196 (inaccurate information) as it applies to a penalty under paragraph 40A of the Schedule." (FA 2026, s.203(2))

"(3) Paragraph 51B(2) and (3) (assessment of a penalty for disclosure) apply to a penalty under section 197 (disclosure of a notice) as they apply to a penalty under paragraph 51B (1) of the Schedule." (FA 2026, s.203(3))

- Paragraph 49 applied to enforcement of the penalties

​

"(4) Paragraph 49 (enforcement of penalty) applies to a penalty under sections 194 to 197 as it applies to a penalty under paragraphs 39, 40 and 40A of the Schedule and, in the case of a penalty under section 197 (disclosure of a notice), the reference to notification under paragraph 46 of the Schedule is to be read as a reference to notification under paragraph 51B(2)(b) of the Schedule)." (FA 2026, s.203(4))

APPEALS

Appealing a notice

​

- Appeal against a connected person notice, or any requirement in it, on any ground

​

"(1) A recipient of a notice under section 179 (connected persons) may appeal against the issue of the notice or any requirement in the notice." (FA 2026, s.204(1))

​

- Appeal against a third party notice only on the ground that compliance would be unduly onerous

​

"(2) A recipient of a notice under section 180 (third parties) may appeal against the issue of the notice or any requirement in the notice on the grounds that it would be unduly onerous to comply with the notice or requirement." (FA 2026, s.204(2))

​

- Appeal against an identification notice only on the ground that compliance would be unduly onerous

​

"(3) A recipient of a notice under section 182 (identification) may appeal against the issue of the notice or any requirement in the notice on the grounds that it would be unduly onerous to comply with the notice or requirement." (FA 2026, s.204(3))

​

- No appeal at all where the notice was issued with tribunal approval

​

"(4) Subsections (1) to (3) do not apply in relation to a notice that was issued with tribunal approval." (FA 2026, s.204(4))

​

- Schedule 36 paragraph 32 applied to appeals against notices

​

"(5) Paragraph 32 of Schedule 36 to FA 2008 applies to appeals under this section as it applies to appeals under Part 5 of that Schedule (and references to information notices are to be read as references to notices under this Chapter)." (FA 2026, s.204(5))

​

Appealing a penalty

​

- Appeal against liability and against amount

​

"(1) A person may appeal against any of the following decisions—

(a) a decision of an officer of Revenue and Customs that a penalty is payable under sections 194 to 197, and

(b) a decision of an officer of Revenue and Customs as to the amount of a penalty under sections 194 to 197." (FA 2026, s.205(1))

​

- Schedule 36 paragraph 48 applied to the procedure on appeal

​

"(2) Paragraph 48 of Schedule 36 to FA 2008 (procedure on appeal against penalty) applies to appeals under this section as it applies to appeals under paragraph 47 of that Schedule (and references to paragraph 47(1)(a) and (b) in paragraph 48 are to be read as references to subsection (1)(a) and (b) of this section). Miscellaneous and interpretation" (FA 2026, s.205(2))

INTERPRETATION AND REPEALS

Interpretation

​

- Definitions applying throughout the Chapter

​

"(1) In this Chapter— “ arrangements ” includes any agreement, scheme, arrangement or understanding of any kind whether or not legally enforceable involving one or more transactions; “ authorised officer of Revenue and Customs ” means an officer of Revenue and Customs who is, or is a member of a class of officers who are, authorised by the Commissioners for the purpose of this Chapter; “ Commissioners ” means the Commissioners for His Majesty’s Revenue and Customs; “ document ” includes any part of a document (and see subsection (2)); “ HMRC ” means His Majesty’s Revenue and Customs; “ information ” includes a document (and see subsection (2)); “ information notice ” means a notice issued under any of sections 179 to 183; “ investigation of tax avoidance ” includes the exercise of a function under an anti-avoidance enactment; “ recipient ”, in relation to a notice, means the person to whom the notice is issued; “ tribunal ” means the First-tier Tribunal or, where determined by or under the Tribunal Procedure Rules, the Upper Tribunal." (FA 2026, s.206(1))

​

- Providing information includes producing documents, and the Schedule 36 provisions about documents are applied

​

"(2) For the purposes of this Chapter—

(a) a reference to providing information includes a reference to producing documents;

(b) a reference to a document is a reference to anything in which information of any description is recorded.

(3) Paragraphs 7 (2) to (4), 8, 15 and 16 of Schedule 36 to FA 2008 (provision relating to documents) apply in relation to documents required under this Chapter as they apply in relation to documents required under that Schedule." (FA 2026, s.206(2), (3))

- Responsibility of officers, want of form, and delivery and service of documents

"(a) section 108 (responsibility of officers);

(b) section 114 (want of form);

(c) section 115 (delivery and service of documents)." (FA 2026, s.207)

"(1) Section 272A of FA 2014 is repealed.

(2) Subsection (1) comes into force on such day as the Treasury may by regulations made by statutory instrument appoint." (FA 2026, s.208)
